The problem

Patients arrive unprepared, and it shows up on the day of surgery.

Too many patients reach the day of surgery unsure about fasting, medicines or tests. The cost lands on them and on the hospital.

37%

of last-minute surgery cancellations are potentially avoidable, often because the patient wasn't ready for her own operation.

British Journal of Anaesthesia, 2026.
How it works

One clear journey, from the first appointment to going home.

Each step shows what is happening, what to do, and what to watch for, using content the hospital has approved.

1

Prepare

Checklists for tests, medicines to pause, and getting fit for surgery.

2

Admission

Fasting times, washing, what to pack and when to arrive.

3

Procedure

What happens in theatre and how she'll feel when she wakes up.

4

Recovery

Moving, eating and pain relief in hospital.

5

Going home

Recovery at home, return to activities and warning signs.

CompanionExample conversation, fictional patient
I'm really nervous about being put to sleep. Could I wake up during the operation?
It's normal to feel nervous about this. Waking up during surgery is very rare, about 1 in 20,000 anaesthetics. Your anaesthetist stays with you the whole time to keep you asleep.
Based on approved information: Could I wake up during surgery?
Can I have my coffee on the morning of surgery?
Yes, if it has no milk. Black coffee counts as a clear drink, and you can have clear drinks until 2 hours before surgery. Your team will tell you the exact time.
Based on approved information: Until when can I drink clear fluids?
Responsible AI by design

The AI explains. The hospital decides what's true.

  • Approved content only. Never general internet knowledge.
  • Sources on every answer. Patients can see where each reply comes from.
  • No guessing. Unanswered questions go to the care team.

Evidence

Patient-facing digital preparation already works.

3.8% → 0.5%

Same-day cancellations fell after a mobile preparation programme for prostate biopsy patients.

Balakrishnan et al., J Med Internet Res 2019. Prospective cohort, 832 patients, UCSF. Read the study
0 of 85

Spine-surgery patients using a preparation app had no last-minute cancellations, against 5 of 89 who didn't, and followed pre-op instructions better.

Stewart et al., Cureus 2019. Non-randomised comparison. Read the study

How a pilot starts

  1. Choose one surgical pathway and a clinical champion.
  2. We build the journey from your protocols; your clinicians approve it.
  3. Agree what success looks like: readiness, calls, day-of cancellations.
